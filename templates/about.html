PROJECT GUIDE

About this project

A learning-focused demonstration of academic data analysis and prediction.

Problem statement

Academic teams may use attendance, prior performance, and learning context to identify students who could benefit from additional support. This application demonstrates how a dataset can be summarized and a simple classifier evaluated for that limited purpose.

Objectives

  • Summarize actual records from a provided CSV dataset.
  • Explore student records with search, filters, and pagination.
  • Train a reproducible baseline classifier and show held-out evaluation results.
  • Present estimates transparently and keep human judgment central.

Technology stack

Flask, Python, Pandas, scikit-learn, joblib, HTML5, CSS3, vanilla JavaScript, and Chart.js.

Data preparation and methodology

The app validates the required schema, missing values, numeric ranges, categorical fields, and target labels. The training script removes exact duplicate records before splitting, reserves a fixed-seed test set, and uses a scikit-learn preprocessing pipeline with one-hot encoding for categorical features and a Random Forest classifier. student_id, final_score, and the target academic_risk are excluded from model inputs.

Evaluation

The model page reports accuracy, macro-averaged precision, recall, F1-score, a confusion matrix, and per-class results on the held-out test set. No test-set tuning is performed by the training script.

Limitations

  • Synthetic labels may encode artificial rules and do not demonstrate real-world validity.
  • Model scores are not calibrated probabilities of academic failure.
  • Bias, missing context, data quality, and changes over time can affect results.
  • This tool must not be used by itself to make consequential decisions about students.
  • External validation, careful governance, and input from students and educators are needed before any real-world use.

Future improvements

Possible extensions include external validation, probability calibration, fairness analysis, temporal evaluation, richer contextual review, and student-centered privacy and consent controls.
